# Track 7: Which text models watermark their output (as of 2026-10-01)

## Headline

1. Exactly two text surfaces carry a statistical watermark today. Google's Gemini app and web apply SynthID-Text, and Anthropic's newer Claude models apply a SynthID-Text variant across every surface, worldwide. Everything else is clean: OpenAI/ChatGPT ships no text watermark, xAI/Grok ships none, Mistral ships none, and no Chinese provider ships one.

2. The Claude watermark applies to only 8 of 15 current model IDs. Claude Fable 5, Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 4.8, Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5 and Sonnet 5.5 are watermarked. Claude Opus 4.5, 4.6, 4.7, Sonnet 4.5, 4.6, Haiku 4.5 and Mythos 5 are not, yet. Anthropic is rolling the mark back to the older models with no published completion date, so the safe Claude set is shrinking.

3. Text watermarking is always a sampling-time logits process, never baked into the weights. No shipped model carries a weight-embedded text watermark. Running Qwen, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek or Gemma locally through Ollama, vLLM or LM Studio produces unmarked text by default, because the watermark is only applied when someone deliberately attaches a logits processor to the sampler.

4. SynthID-Text, and therefore Claude's variant, is easy to remove. An off-the-shelf paraphraser scrubs it with over 90 percent success on 1,000-token text; a black-box stealing attack pushes that near 100 percent. Translation and rewriting by another model break it, and it is sparse on factual or low-entropy text to begin with.

5. Neither China nor the EU forces a watermark onto a solo author's local workflow. China's 2025-09-01 measures require visible labels and file metadata, and merely encourage digital watermarks. The EU's Article 50 marks providers, not deployers, and its only text duty on a publisher (public-interest text) carries an editorial-responsibility exemption.

## Findings

### 1. Who watermarks text, on which surface, since when

**Google Gemini (SynthID-Text).** SynthID-Text is a production text watermark described in a Nature paper published 2024-10-23, and it has watermarked Gemini and Gemini Advanced in the consumer app and web experience since then. The Nature abstract states that non-distortionary SynthID-Text "has been used to watermark Gemini and Gemini Advanced," based on a live experiment across nearly 20 million Gemini responses. Source: Dathathri et al., Nature 634:818-823, 2024-10-23, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4.

The critical surface distinction is that the watermark lives in the app and web, not the developer API. The SRI Lab at ETH Zurich tested this directly and reported on 2024-12-20 that "we found no reliable evidence of a watermark on the Gemini 1.5 API," which "matches the official claims, stating that the watermark is only present in the Gemini App and Web." Source: SRI Lab, "Probing Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text Watermark," 2024-12-20, https://www.sri.inf.ethz.ch/blog/probingsynthid. Google's own developer documentation frames SynthID as a tool you attach yourself: it is "a logits processor, applied to your model's generation pipeline," that you pass to the model's `.generate()` method. Source: Google AI for Developers, "SynthID: Tools for watermarking and detecting LLM-generated Text," https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid. So Gemini API output is not automatically watermarked; the watermark is a property of the Gemini consumer surfaces.

**Anthropic Claude (SynthID-Text variant).** Anthropic announced on 2026-08-14 that Claude models launched on or after 2026-08-02 watermark text with a SynthID-Text variant, applied globally at launch because Anthropic could not yet scope it by region. The mark applies at the model level across all surfaces: the API, claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, Claude Tag, and cloud hosting on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. Detection is via a private-preview API open only to regulators, media, fact-checkers, researchers and enterprises. Source: Anthropic, "How Claude's text watermark works," 2026-08-14, https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark.

The exact model list is in Anthropic's support article. Watermarked (text watermark on first-party surfaces): Claude Fable 5.1, Fable 5, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5, Opus 5, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5.5, Sonnet 5. Not yet watermarked: Claude Mythos 5, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.6, Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5. Source: Anthropic, "How Claude marks AI-generated content," accessed 2026-10-01, https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content. Anthropic describes this as "working to add marking support for earlier Claude models" with no published date, so the unwatermarked set is in transition.

Two practical properties matter for our use case. First, the watermark is sparse where the model has no free choice: factual statements, code, and proofreading put almost nothing for the mark to attach to. Second, when Claude merely edits human text, nearly all words are the human's, so the mark is thin to absent. Both points are in the Anthropic announcement above.

**OpenAI (ChatGPT / GPT-5.x).** OpenAI built a text watermark around 2023-2024, rated it roughly 99.9 percent effective internally, then shelved it. The Wall Street Journal reported the internal build in August 2024, and OpenAI confirmed it and explained the reasons: the mark degrades under translation and rewriting, and it would disproportionately flag non-native English speakers who use AI as a writing aid. Through GPT-5, ChatGPT ships no text watermark. Source: writehuman.ai, "Does ChatGPT Watermark Text? What OpenAI Shipped in 2026," 2026-09-15, https://writehuman.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-watermark-text. A vendor-neutral comparison reaches the same conclusion and lists OpenAI text watermarking as "not confirmed (verify)" while confirming C2PA and SynthID on images and audio. Source: Layer3 Labs, "Which AI Models Watermark Their Output? (2026)," updated 2026-09-19, https://www.layer3labs.io/comparisons/which-ai-models-watermark-their-output.

OpenAI did sign the EU Code of Practice and has stated, "consistent with our commitments under the European Commission's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated content, our goal is to expand provenance signals to all modalities including text." That is intent, not a shipped watermark. Source: City AM, "ChatGPT might follow Claude's watermark pledge but Grok to swerve it," 2026, https://www.cityam.com/chatgpt-might-follow-claudes-watermark-pledge-but-grok-to-swerve-it/.

**xAI (Grok).** xAI did not sign the Code of Practice and has made no watermark commitment for Grok. No primary source confirms a shipped text watermark. It remains subject to Article 50 regardless, but today Grok text is unmarked. Source: City AM (same article) and Layer3 Labs (same comparison).

**Mistral.** Mistral signed the Code of Practice, but has not publicly confirmed a shipped text watermark as of September 2026. Source: City AM (signatory) and Layer3 Labs ("not confirmed").

**DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Moonshot Kimi, Zhipu GLM, MiniMax.** None ships a statistical in-text watermark. The Layer3 Labs comparison lists DeepSeek as "not confirmed" and open-weight, noting an operator running DeepSeek's weights locally decides whether any marking layer runs at all. The Chinese labeling regime (below) requires visible labels and metadata, not in-text statistical watermarks, and there is no primary source showing any of these vendors embedding one. DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM all ship open weights, which makes any such mark trivially avoidable by self-hosting.

**Gateways (OpenRouter, OpenCode Zen/Go).** These are resellers that route requests to the upstream provider. The watermark is applied at the provider's sampling layer, so a gateway passes it through unchanged: it neither adds nor strips a mark. OpenCode Zen's documentation lists the provider model IDs it proxies (including the watermarked Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, and the unwatermarked Opus 4.5) and states that OpenAI and Anthropic API requests are retained under each provider's own data policies, confirming the call reaches the actual provider. Source: OpenCode, "Zen," https://opencode.ai/docs/zen/. The practical rule is that the upstream model decides, not the gateway: Claude Opus 4.5 via any gateway is clean today; Claude Opus 5.5 via any gateway is watermarked.

### 2. China's AI-generated content labeling measures (effective 2025-09-01)

The Measures for Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content, jointly issued by the CAC, MIIT, MPS and SARFT, took effect 2025-09-01. They define two kinds of label. Explicit labels are perceivable: for text, that means a text notification or common symbol "at appropriate positions in the beginning, end, or middle of text." Implicit labels are technical marks added to "generated synthetic content file data," which the measures define as file metadata in the file header, and which must include the content attribute, the provider name or code, and a reference number. Source: China Law Translate, "Measures for Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content," promulgated 2025-03-07, https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/ai-labeling/.

The key sentence for us is Article 5: "Service providers are encouraged to add implicit labels to generated synthetic content in forms such as digital watermarks." Encouraged, not required. The mandatory implicit label is metadata on files, not a statistical signal woven into the words. So the Chinese regime does not require, and in practice has not produced, an in-text statistical watermark like SynthID-Text. For text, the obligation is a visible "AI-generated" marker plus metadata on exported files, and Article 9 lets a provider deliver content without explicit labels after the user accepts a service agreement, with six months of logs.

Scope: the measures bind "online information service providers" under China's deep-synthesis and generative-AI rules, meaning the Chinese companies themselves. A foreign author using a Chinese API is not the regulated party. The provider's own surfaces (for example the DeepSeek app) carry the visible labels; raw API text outside China is not itself a "file" with a header, so the metadata requirement does not bite, and there is no evidence of any statistical watermark being added to API text. The mandatory national standards set the technical format of the implicit labels, but again for metadata, not in-text statistics.

### 3. EU AI Act Article 50 and the Code of Practice

Article 50(2) obliges providers of generative AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, to ensure outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated. It applies from 2026-08-02; systems already on the market before that date get until 2026-12-02 under the May 2026 AI Omnibus agreement. Article 50(2) carries an assistive-function carve-out: it does not apply where the system performs only an assistive editing function (for example grammar correction) or does not substantially alter the input or its semantics. Article 50(4) separately obliges deployers to disclose AI-generated text published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest, unless it has undergone human review and a person holds editorial responsibility. Source: Future of Life Institute, "The EU AI Act's Transparency Rules: A Practical Guide to Article 50," 2026-05-14, https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/.

The Commission's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content (final version published 2026-06-10) is the voluntary tool for demonstrating compliance. About 190 organisations had signed by the end of July 2026. Source: European Commission, "Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content," last updated 2026-07-31, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content.

Two points bear on us directly. First, who is a provider. A provider is a person who develops an AI system and places it on the market or puts it into service. A solo author running an open-weight model on their own machine is not a provider; the marking obligation in Article 50(2) does not fall on them. The "open-source systems are not exempt" line means that a company shipping an open-weight model as a provider cannot escape Article 50; it does not convert a local user into a provider. Second, the draft Code's treatment of open-weight models. The first draft suggested open-weight model providers "should implement structural marking techniques encoded in the weights during training" to help downstream compliance, but that was a proposal in a draft, not a requirement in the final rule, and no such weight-encoded text watermark exists in practice. Source: Cooley, "EU AI Act: First Draft Code of Practice on Transparency and Watermarking Released," 2025-12-18, https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-12-18-eu-ai-act-first-draft-code-of-practice-on-transparency-and-watermarking-released.

So under EU law, the author's own local models and any hosted models are not subject to a watermark he must carry; the only residual duty is the deployer label on public-interest text, with the editorial-responsibility exemption, which an honestly attributed, human-reviewed blog post meets.

### 4. Open-weight models and weight-embedded watermarks

SynthID-Text "does not affect LLM training and modifies only the sampling procedure." Source: Nature paper abstract, 2024-10-23. Google's documentation calls it "a logits processor" with "no additional training required." Source: ai.google.dev SynthID docs. The entire deployed watermarking literature, from Aaronson's 2022 proposal through Kirchenbauer's red-green scheme to SynthID-Text, is a sampling-time modification: a pseudorandom scoring function biases token selection, and detection measures the correlation between the chosen tokens and a secret key.

This means a watermark cannot survive in the weights of an open-weight model in the way image watermarks survive in pixels. The weights define the token distribution; the watermark is introduced by the sampler. Run Qwen, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek or Gemma through Ollama, vLLM or LM Studio with the stock sampler and you get unmarked text, because nothing in those frameworks attaches a watermarking logits processor unless the operator deliberately adds one (for example by enabling Hugging Face's `SynthIDTextWatermarkLogitsProcessor`, which ships in Transformers v4.46.0+ as an opt-in tool).

Is there any evidence of a weight-embedded text watermark in a shipped model? No. The Nature paper surveys the alternative approaches and dismisses data-driven watermarking, the one family that touches training, because it relies on trigger phrases and only flags specific misuse, not general text. The Cooley memo records the EU draft's aspiration for weight-embedded structural marking, which confirms it does not exist yet. There is no published model whose weights carry a general-purpose text watermark that fires on arbitrary prose. For our purposes the conclusion is crisp: local inference is watermark-free by construction, not by luck.

### 5. Robustness of SynthID-Text to translation, paraphrase and editing

The Nature paper's own supplementary material (section C.6) already signals weakness: under scrubbing by paraphrase, the watermark AUC drops to about 0.7 for 1,000-token texts. Source: SRI Lab, referencing the SynthID-Text supplementary, 2024-12-20.

The SRI Lab evaluation gives the hard numbers. Scrubbing, measured as the fraction of paraphrases that are both high-quality and classified unwatermarked at a 0.1 percent false-positive rate, exceeds 90 percent for SynthID-Text even with a naive off-the-shelf paraphraser (DIPPER), and reaches near 100 percent when the attacker first uses black-box queries to steal the watermark. By contrast, baseline red-green watermarks are much harder to scrub without stealing (2 to 26 percent). Spoofing goes the other way: SynthID-Text is harder to forge than comparable schemes, at 4 to 9 percent spoofing success versus above 80 percent for SOTA red-green, though tripling the query budget raises it to 15 percent. The same group also showed that the mere presence of a SynthID-Text watermark is trivially detectable from black-box queries, which is a separate problem from removal. Source: SRI Lab, 2024-12-20, https://www.sri.inf.ethz.ch/blog/probingsynthid.

Anthropic's own announcement adds the qualitative picture: the watermark is sparser on factual passages, proofreading and code because there is less free choice for the mark to act on, and detection "doesn't work well on small samples." Source: Anthropic, 2026-08-14. OpenAI's stated reason for shelving its own watermark is the same mechanism: translation into another language and back, or a rewrite by a second model, weakens the mark, which is why OpenAI declined to ship it. Source: writehuman.ai, 2026-09-15.

The bottom line for robustness: translation and paraphrasing by a second model reliably break SynthID-Text; heavy human editing does too, and the mark is weak on exactly the factual, low-entropy prose a careful author writes.

## Recommendation

Safe model families for final prose today: everything except Google's Gemini consumer surfaces and Anthropic's watermarked Claude IDs. Concretely, use OpenAI GPT-5.x or ChatGPT, xAI Grok, Mistral, or any Chinese provider, all of which ship unmarked text. For Claude, the older IDs (Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5/4.6, Haiku 4.5) are still unwatermarked, but that set is explicitly shrinking, so pin the ID rather than relying on "Claude" generically. For absolute certainty, run an open-weight model locally: Qwen, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek or Gemma through Ollama, vLLM or LM Studio applies no watermark unless you opt into a logits processor, and no shipped model carries a weight-embedded text mark.

If a watermarked model must draft an earlier stage, three transformations reliably strip the upstream mark. First, a full rewrite by a different, unwatermarked model, which is OpenAI's own stated reason the mark is fragile. Second, translation to another language and back, which the same OpenAI reasoning and the SRI scrubbing numbers both support. Third, heavy human editing, which works because the mark lives only in the word choices and is sparse on factual or low-entropy text. Note that the last two also align with the humanization goal: rewriting into the author's voice is exactly what a humanization pass does, so the watermark removal and the de-slopping step collapse into one operation. Do not rely on proofreading, on short excerpts, or on code for watermark presence; there is little mark there to begin with.

## Open questions

- Whether Google has since expanded SynthID-Text to the Gemini developer API. The evidence is from 2024-12 and says no; Google's own docs still present SynthID as opt-in tooling, but I could not find a current, dated statement on the API default in 2026.
- The exact completion date for Anthropic's rollback to older Claude models. Anthropic says "open-ended" with no date, so the unwatermarked Claude IDs are a moving target.
- Whether OpenAI will actually ship the text watermark its support page promises, and when. The statement is intent, not a shipped artifact.
- Whether any Chinese provider adds a statistical watermark to raw API text outside its app. The law does not require it and no primary source shows it, but none of the vendors publishes a definitive "we do not watermark text" statement.
